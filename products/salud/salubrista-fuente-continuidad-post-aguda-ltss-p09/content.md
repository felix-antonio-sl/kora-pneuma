# Crónicas del Crepúsculo Geriátrico - Parte 09

## Professional Organizations and the Medical Director Role (AMDA — Table 4)

- [P0473] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must help ensure appropriate physician coverage and provision of medical services → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0474] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must help develop/implement system to monitor practitioner performance and give feedback → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0475] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must help protect resident rights including advance care planning and ethical issues → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0476] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must advise on infection control issues, approve specific infection control policies, evaluate infection control practices → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0477] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must help promote employee health/safety including implementation of employee health policies/programs → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0478] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must promote learning culture within facility → `AMDA Table 4`
- [P0479] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must be aware of social, regulatory, political, economic factors affecting medical/health services of LTC residents and short-stay patients → `AMDA Table 4`

## Medical Director Relationship with Practitioners

- [P0480] **REGLA** — Per OBRA'87 surveyor guidance, practitioners are responsible to medical director for their performance and practice
- [P0481] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must clarify practitioner responsibilities and performance expectations: regulatory visit frequency, facility policies, clinical standards of practice
- [P0482] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director must help facility develop process to review physician/practitioner credentials and address/resolve concerns between physicians, practitioners, and staff

## Practitioner Responsibilities

- [P0483] **REGLA** — Medical director clarifies practitioner responsibilities by: (1) setting expectations, (2) explaining how to fulfill them, (3) establishing satisfactory performance criteria, (4) determining whether expectations met, (5) giving feedback
- [P0484] **HECHO** — Medical director can guide staff/practitioners on ADEs, common causes of acute condition changes, falls, altered mental status, decline in function
- [P0485] **HECHO** — LTC medicine = hybrid of ambulatory, office-based, and hospital-based practice
- [P0486] **HECHO** — Individuals of other disciplines often serve as intermediaries between patient and physician (assessment, monitoring, identifying problems, conveying concerns)
- [P0487] **HECHO** — Assisted Living facilities typically have less organized structure and fewer direct care staff than nursing homes

## The Medical Director's Relationship with the Facility

- [P0488] **HECHO** — Administrator, Director of Nursing, medical director = key management leadership team in NF
- [P0489] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Leadership team ultimately responsible for successful implementation of facility care processes/practices
- [P0490] **TENSIÓN** — Unlike medical staff president/chief of staff (represents physicians), medical director in LTC fulfills pivotal role for facility and its practitioners
- [P0491] **HECHO** — Medical director performance varies considerably across country; many facilities challenged to find qualified attending physicians and medical directors
- [P0492] **REGLA** — OBRA '87 surveyor guidance requires medical director be currently licensed as physician in state where facility located (unless exception warranted)
- [P0493] **PERMISO** — Facility may employ or contract directly with medical director, or contract with company/academic program employing physician
- [P0494] **PERMISO** — In multi-facility organizations, arrangements may be made by corporate/regional offices with policies at corporate level; medical director still must guide facility to follow appropriate clinical policies/procedures
- [P0495] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Facility must maintain environment supporting competent clinical practice
- [P0496] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Before contacting practitioner, staff should be trained to: perform proper assessments, coordinate phone calls, provide accurate situation description, be prepared to answer physician questions, know when to notify medical director

## The Medical Director's Role in Facility Quality

- [P0497] **HECHO** — Medicare Triple Aim: improve patient outcomes, improve patient satisfaction/safety, provide relevant care using resources effectively at lower cost
- [P0498] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director should help facility identify/address potentially remediable issues including impact of physician/practitioner performance and practices
- [P0499] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director should learn regulatory expectations by reviewing CMS State Operations Manual (SOM) for surveyors
- [P0500] **HECHO** — CMS "Nursing Home Compare" website reports quality measures data based on MDS, staffing, and compliance (survey deficiencies) → `medicare.gov/nursinghomecompare`
- [P0501] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Medical director should engage in in-depth case reviews with other disciplines looking for care quality/safety issues not identified by CMS quality measures
- [P0502] **PERMISO** — When needed, medical director may intervene directly in care of other physicians' patients by examining patient and reviewing orders
- [P0503] **REGLA** — Medical director may intervene if practitioner does not respond appropriately/timely to notification of significant acute condition change or inadequately supervises care by IDT members
- [P0504] **REGLA** — Medical director accountable to facility administrator; licensed health care practitioners/attending physicians accountable to medical director

## Introduction

- [P0505] **HECHO** — National effort ongoing to assist older adults maintain highest functional status and age in place, aided by telemedicine, remote monitoring, extended home health, home-based primary care models
- [P0506] **HECHO** — NF/assisted living residents often most vulnerable due to multi-morbidities, neurocognitive disorders, psychosocial issues
- [P0507] **DEFINICIÓN** — Nurse practitioners (NP) = Registered Nurses with Master of Nursing (MSN) or Doctor of Nursing Practice (DNP)
- [P0508] **HECHO** — DNP established to acknowledge professional parity with pharmacists and physical therapists who received clinical doctorate
- [P0509] **HECHO** — NPs with DNP have additional education/clinical training in quality improvement, leadership, health policy
- [P0510] **HECHO** — NPs practicing in NFs usually designated as gerontological, adult, adult-gerontological, or family NP
- [P0511] **HECHO** — Clinical Nurse Specialist (CNS) education in psychiatric mental health changed to psychiatric mental health NP
- [P0512] **HECHO** — NPs have >6 years academic/clinical training
- [P0513] **HECHO** — NPs and CNS shown to improve several health measures, resident behavior, family satisfaction in LTC
- [P0514] **HECHO** — NP/CNS = autonomous and collaborative IDT members enhancing accessibility/quality of care in NFs
- [P0515] **HECHO** — >355,000 NPs licensed in USA; full-time NPs increased from 14% (2008) → 36% (2018)
- [P0516] **HECHO** — NPs represent 60% of full-time primary care practitioners in LTC sector
- [P0517] **ALCANCE** — NPs trained to: perform histories/physicals, diagnose/treat acute illness, manage chronic conditions, order/interpret labs and diagnostic reports (X-rays, Doppler, cardiac studies), prescribe medications including controlled substances (state-dependent), provide health teaching/anticipatory guidance/supportive counseling
- [P0518] **HECHO** — As of 2022: 24 states = full practice (NPs evaluate, diagnose, order/interpret tests, initiate/manage treatment including prescribing under state board of nursing authority)
- [P0519] **HECHO** — Full practice model recommended by Institute of Medicine and National Council of State Boards of Nursing
- [P0520] **HECHO** — 16 states = reduced practice (require collaborative agreement with another health discipline, usually physician, for ≥1 element of NP practice)
- [P0521] **HECHO** — 11 states = restricted practice (require supervision, delegation, or team-management by outside health entity/practitioner for ≥1 element of NP practice)
- [P0522] **HECHO** — IOM report "The Future of Nursing: Leading Change, Advancing Health" acknowledges nurses should practice to full extent of education/training/scope of practice and be full practice partners with physicians

## Partnering with a Nurse Practitioner or Physician Assistant

- [P0523] **HECHO** — NPs can achieve better resident health care goals without increased cost of care
- [P0524] **HECHO** — NPs and PAs practicing in LTC can reduce hospital admissions and costs
- [P0525] **HECHO** — Evercare program: NFs with NP managing resident care had half the hospitalizations vs NFs without NP → `Evercare`
- [P0526] **HECHO** — Meta-analyses/systematic reviews: NP practice in LTC associated with high patient satisfaction, decreased hospitalizations, decreased all-cause mortality vs physician-alone practice
- [P0527] **HECHO** — NP practice in LTC associated with reduced ED transfers
- [P0528] **HECHO** — NP practice in LTC associated with lower rates of depression, urinary incontinence, pressure ulcers, restraint use, less aggressive behavior
- [P0529] **HECHO** — NPs practice with high degree of collaboration with interprofessional team
- [P0530] **HECHO** — NPs provide leadership activities: education of IDT staff in quality improvement, evidence-based practice, practice innovation

## Clinical Nurse Specialists

- [P0531] **DEFINICIÓN** — CNS = advanced practice nurses with Master's degree in nursing, often further specialized training in particular clinical nursing area
- [P0532] **HECHO** — CNS most frequently employed in Magnet hospitals
- [P0533] **HECHO** — ~89,000 practicing CNS in United States
- [P0534] **ALCANCE** — CNS = experts in diagnosis/treatment of illness, health promotion, implementation of evidence-based practice
- [P0535] **HECHO** — CNS shown to reduce urinary incontinence, pressure ulcers, aggressive behavior, use of restraints (physical and pharmacological), fall-related injuries, and improve effect on cognitively impaired residents
- [P0536] **HECHO** — Adult/gerontological CNSs expanding practice in wound care, quality improvement, education/leadership roles within LTC
- [P0537] **HECHO** — Psychiatric mental health CNS and NPs trained in behavioral management of persons with dementia and serious mental illness

## Physician Assistants

- [P0538] **HECHO** — >132,940 PAs currently working in USA; number expected to increase
- [P0539] **HECHO** — 2013 AAPA national survey: 0.9% of PAs directly employed by NF or other LTC facility
- [P0540] **HECHO** — PA training programs commonly based at medical schools, hospitals, other health care facilities
- [P0541] **HECHO** — PA program = 26 months: 1st year = basic medical sciences, followed by clinical rotations in all major medical specialties
- [P0542] **HECHO** — PAs have >2000 hours clinical/skill training
- [P0543] **REQUISITO** — PA must pass Physician Assistant National Certifying Exam (PANCE) to practice
- [P0544] **REQUISITO** — PA requires continuing education to recertify every 10 years
- [P0545] **DEFINICIÓN** — PA scope of practice defined by 4 domains: education/experience, state law, facility policy, patient needs
- [P0546] **HECHO** — PAs expected to perform patient care tasks at similar skill level/competency as physician
- [P0547] **HECHO** — State boards do not require PA to be proficient in any specific tasks
- [P0548] **HECHO** — Collaboration does not necessarily require constant physician presence; in rural areas PA can be primary care provider with supervising physician checking in 1-2×/week
- [P0549] **HECHO** — Significant lack of PAs practicing in LTC despite 20% working in primary care; PAs prefer orthopedics, urgent care, inpatient care

## Prescribing Privileges of PAs and NPs

- [P0550] **REGLA** — All states allow PAs to prescribe if physician either directly involved or available
- [P0551] **HECHO** — PA prescribing association: delegation (19 states), authorization (18 states), other requiring physician directly involved in supervisory role (13 states), collaboration (Arizona only)
- [P0552] **HECHO** — 24 states allow NPs prescriptive authority to diagnose/treat patients independently; remaining states require varying levels of collaboration/delegation/supervision
- [P0553] **REGLA** — Prescribing of scheduled drugs regulated/enforced by individual states through DEA
- [P0554] **DEFINICIÓN** — Schedule I: no medical use, research only, highest abuse potential (heroin, marijuana, LSD, MDMA)
- [P0555] **DEFINICIÓN** — Schedule II/IIN: severe psychic/physical dependence, high abuse potential (opium, codeine, hydromorphone, methadone, hydrocodone, amphetamine, methamphetamine)
- [P0556] **DEFINICIÓN** — Schedule III/IIIN: moderate/low physical/psychical dependency, high abuse potential (acetaminophen+codeine, buprenorphine, ketamine, anabolic steroids)
- [P0557] **DEFINICIÓN** — Schedule IV: limited psychological/physical dependency, low abuse potential (chlordiazepoxide, diazepam, barbital, phenobarbital, alprazolam)
- [P0558] **DEFINICIÓN** — Schedule V: OTC or Rx with limited narcotics for analgesic/antitussive/antidiarrheal use, lowest abuse potential (buprenorphine, propylhexedrine)
- [P0559] **REGLA** — Controlled drug prescribing allowed for PAs in majority of states through collaborative practice; 15 states restrict to Schedule III-V
- [P0560] **EXCLUSIÓN** — Florida and Kentucky do not allow any controlled drugs to be prescribed by PA
- [P0561] **REGLA** — All states except Florida allow NPs to prescribe controlled substances; majority allow Schedule II-V; 8 states restrict to Schedule III-V
- [P0562] **REGLA** — DEA designates NPs/PAs as "mid-level practitioners"; DEA registration requires completing all state requirements + state license; DEA number begins with "M"
