# Crónicas del Crepúsculo Geriátrico - Parte 17

## Introduction

- [P1215] **HECHO** — CAPC reports up to 80% of NF residents could benefit from palliative care
- [P1216] **REQUISITO** — As many facilities lack on-site specialist palliative care consultation, practitioners must acquire competency to integrate palliative care into daily practice
- [P1217] **HECHO** — Core palliative care challenge areas: advance care planning (prognostication/values clarification), symptom management, psychosocial support (Kristler, Sloane, Zimmerman)
- [P1218] **REGLA** — Palliative care can supplement traditional medical care; disease-modifying treatments not necessarily abandoned if congruent with patient values/preferences/goals
- [P1219] **HECHO** — F-Tag 684 = Quality of Care (hospice/palliative/terminally ill); F-Tag 697 = Pain Management; F-Tag 849 = Hospice Services; F-Tag 744 = Care of residents with dementia; F-Tag 757 = Unnecessary Drug Use
- [P1220] **DEFINICIÓN** — CMS Quadruple Aim: improve health of patient populations, enhance patient/family experience, reduce healthcare costs, improve job satisfaction

## Guiding Principles of Palliative Care

- [P1221] **DEFINICIÓN** — Core principles of palliative care: reduce symptom burden/suffering; address physical/psycho-emotional/social/spiritual dimensions; provide goal-congruent medical treatment; promote interdisciplinary care; assist with ethical/legal issues
- [P1222] **DEFINICIÓN** — Primary palliative care goals for patient/family: highest practical quality of life, autonomy/dignity, relieve suffering, alleviate family burden, mend estranged relationships

## Illness Trajectories and Prognostication

- [P1223] **HECHO** — Leading causes of death in US: heart disease, cancer, stroke, lung disease, Alzheimer's, influenza/pneumonia, kidney disease, unintentional injuries, diabetes, septicemia, COVID-19
- [P1224] **HECHO** — Common NF death causes: 30-60% cardiovascular, 1-23% pulmonary, 36% Alzheimer's/dementia (10% of all dementia deaths in NFs), 7-9% cancer, 2-3% ESRD
- [P1225] **HECHO** — As of June 2021: CMS reported 657,457 resident COVID-19 confirmed cases and 133,210 resident deaths in NFs; 586,899 staff confirmed cases and 1,949 staff deaths
- [P1226] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 illness trajectories leading to death: (1) short rapid decline weeks/months (cancer), (2) prolonged dwindling over years (dementia/Parkinson's/MS/ALS), (3) progressive functional decline 2-5 years with intermittent acute episodes/partial recovery (cardiac/pulmonary), (4) acute catastrophic event death within 7-14 days (stroke/MI/organ failure)
- [P1227] **HECHO** — Flacker Mortality Score provides % probability of death in 1 year in newly admitted NF residents ≥65
- [P1228] **HECHO** — MDS-based prognostic characteristics: significant weight loss, swallowing problems, BMI <22, age >88, CHF, shortness of breath, male sex
- [P1229] **HECHO** — Palliative Prognostic Score may predict 30-day survival; delirium = significant prognostic factor
- [P1230] **HECHO** — Symptom prevalence in advanced illness: fatigue 72-74% cancer/76% non-cancer; pain 71-74% cancer/67% non-cancer; breathlessness 36% cancer and non-cancer/60-95% COPD/heart/renal; anxiety/depression 40% cancer/57% non-cancer

## General Indicators and Clinical Parameters of Serious Illness

- [P1231] **HECHO** — Indicators patient may die within 6-12 months: repeated ER visits/frequent hospitalizations, recurrent life-threatening infections, PPS <50-70% or increasing dependency in ≥2 BADLs
- [P1232] **HECHO** — Additional indicators: progressive unavoidable weight loss 5% in 3 months or 10% in 6 months, serum albumin <2.5 gm/dL, ≥1 stage 3/4 pressure ulcers, BMI <22 (especially <20), age >88, severe refractory symptoms (fatigue, dyspnea, peripheral edema, bowel obstruction)
- [P1233] **OBLIGACIÓN** — Important to determine patient/surrogate decision-making capacity for healthcare, practical, and financial matters
- [P1234] **HECHO** — Advanced healthcare directives may be state-specific and may not apply in state where patient now resides
- [P1235] **DEFINICIÓN** — Ethical principles of healthcare: autonomy (self-determination), beneficence (do good), nonmaleficence (avoid harm), justice (treat fairly/equally/equitably), fidelity (truthfulness, dignity, non-abandonment)

## Symptom Assessment and Management

- [P1236] **HECHO** — Non-pain symptoms often more prevalent than pain even in cancer patients
- [P1237] **REQUISITO** — Symptom assessment steps: establish intensity/temporal pattern/exacerbating-relieving factors/location/effect on function; determine acute/chronic/intermittent; identify associated symptoms; assess QOL/ADL/cognition/sleep/mood impact; review treatments; focused physical exam; ascertain pathophysiology; check if medication-caused; identify reversible causes; least invasive testing; evaluate psycho-emotional/spiritual/social factors; initiate treatment based on illness phase/prognosis/preferences
- [P1238] **HECHO** — Assessment scales by symptom: Anorexia=FAACT; Anxiety=HAM-A; Cognition=MMSE/COGNISTAT/MOCA; Constipation=Modified Constipation Assessment Scale; Delirium=CAM/DRS; Depression=Beck/GDS/Zung/Cornell/CES-D; Dyspnea=NAS/VAS; Pain=NAS/VAS/Wong-Baker/FLACC/BPI/PAINAD; Spiritual=FICA/Herth Hope Index
- [P1239] **REGLA** — Interventions to relieve symptoms can lead to unintended consequences (double effect); reasonable to continue first-line treatment while initiating treatment for adverse effects
- [P1240] **REGLA** — Palliative sedation indicated for imminently dying patients with intractable suffering unresponsive to other palliative interventions and intolerable to patient (NHPCO 2010)
- [P1241] **HECHO** — ESAS (Edmonton Symptom Assessment Survey) assesses presence/intensity of multiple symptoms tracked over time

## Anorexia/Cachexia

- [P1242] **DEFINICIÓN** — Anorexia = lack of appetite; cachexia = catabolic state with severe muscle loss ± loss of adipose tissue; occur in late-stage progressive illness
- [P1243] **REGLA** — Appetite stimulants: corticosteroid (prednisone 5-20 mg/d or dexamethasone 4-8 mg/d), progestin (megestrol max 400-800 mg/d, trial 4-8 weeks), mirtazapine (off-label, also mood enhancer, give at bedtime)
- [P1244] **HECHO** — Corticosteroid appetite effect often decreases after several weeks; megestrol can cause lower limb edema and increase thrombophlebitis/thromboembolism risk
- [P1245] **HECHO** — Insufficient evidence to recommend cannabinoids (dronabinol), cyproheptadine, oxandrolone, or thalidomide for anorexia
- [P1246] **REGLA** — Prefer lactose-free nutritional supplements especially in elderly/African Americans (higher lactose intolerance prevalence)

## Dyspnea

- [P1247] **DEFINICIÓN** — Dyspnea = discomfort in breathing including breathlessness/shortness of breath/increased work of breathing; often not associated with tachypnea or hypoxemia
- [P1248] **REGLA** — First-line pharmacotherapy for dyspnea palliation: immediate-release opioid q3-4h; opioid-naive start morphine 2.5-5 mg PO or OME equivalent; titrate upward 25-50% q12-24h
- [P1249] **DEFINICIÓN** — Opioid-tolerant patient = taking consecutive oral daily MME ≥60 mg for 7-10 days
- [P1250] **REGLA** — For excessive oral/respiratory secretions: hyoscyamine PO/SL, glycopyrrolate PO, atropine ophthalmic SL, scopolamine SL or patch q3 days; guaifenesin for thick secretions
- [P1251] **REGLA** — Consider low-dose benzodiazepine (preferably lorazepam) for breakthrough/refractory dyspnea as anxiety often contributing factor
- [P1252] **HECHO** — Scheduled opioid (± benzodiazepine) better than PRN for dyspnea; opioids effective for COPD dyspnea but may be less effective for cancer/heart failure dyspnea
- [P1253] **REGLA** — When using opioid for dyspnea, aim for respiratory rate 14-20
- [P1254] **HECHO** — Oxygen may reverse hypoxemia but dyspnea may not improve; potential for oxygen to cause hypercapnia/obtundation/respiratory arrest

## Nausea and Vomiting

- [P1255] **DEFINICIÓN** — 4 main pathophysiologic mechanisms of emetic reflex: cortical, vestibular, chemoreceptor trigger zone, gastrointestinal → `Table 3`
- [P1256] **REGLA** — Cortical N/V: tumor/increased ICP → dexamethasone; anxiety → benzodiazepine; pain → opioid/adjuvants
- [P1257] **REGLA** — Vestibular N/V → meclizine, scopolamine, dimenhydrinate
- [P1258] **REGLA** — CTZ-mediated N/V: medications → decrease/stop; metabolic (kidney/liver failure) → haloperidol/olanzapine; hyponatremia → NaCl/demeclocycline; hypercalcemia → bisphosphonate/dexamethasone
- [P1259] **REGLA** — GI N/V: drug-related → stop drug/consider PPI; tumor → promethazine/metoclopramide/octreotide; constipation → bowel regimen; cough-induced → opioid/anticholinergic
- [P1260] **HECHO** — Metoclopramide can cause EPS, dystonia, tardive dyskinesia
- [P1261] **REGLA** — Low-dose haloperidol (0.5-2 mg) or olanzapine (2.5-7.5 mg) may alleviate N/V
- [P1262] **HECHO** — Dronabinol antiemetic: start 2.5 mg BID, max 20 mg/day; FDA-approved for refractory chemo N/V; adverse effects include somnolence, paranoia, delirium

## Constipation

- [P1263] **HECHO** — Constipating drugs include opioids, anticholinergics, iron, calcium, calcium channel blockers, diuretics, clonidine
- [P1264] **REGLA** — Stepwise constipation regimen ("laxative ladder"): (1) senna ± docusate 1-2 tabs qd-bid, (2) senna 3-4 tabs bid, (3) add sorbitol/lactulose 30cc qd-bid or PEG 17g in 8oz water qd-bid, (4) glycerin ± bisacodyl suppository qd-qod, (5) mineral oil or soap suds enema, (6) digital dis-impaction for rectal impaction
- [P1265] **REGLA** — Stimulant laxatives (senna) most effective for opioid-induced constipation; as opioid dose increases, laxative regimen must also increase
- [P1266] **REGLA** — Bulk-forming laxatives NOT recommended; can exacerbate constipation in underhydrated/less mobile patients
- [P1267] **HECHO** — Rectal fecal impaction can cause "paradoxical" diarrhea or urinary retention
- [P1268] **REGLA** — Life-threatening causes to identify: malignant bowel obstruction, narcotic bowel syndrome

## Delirium

- [P1269] **DEFINICIÓN** — Delirium = acute confusional state with fluctuating course, inattention, disorganized thinking/speech; can be hyperactive, hypoactive, or mixed
- [P1270] **REGLA** — Any acute illness or any medication (regardless of when started) can precipitate delirium; gradual dose reduction/discontinuation of opioid/benzodiazepine/alcohol can also precipitate delirium
- [P1271] **REGLA** — First-line delirium medication: low-dose haloperidol (≤2-3 mg total/day) in divided doses; start low, go slow
- [P1272] **REGLA** — Second-line: low-dose lorazepam 0.5-1 mg PO/SL q6-8h; or valproic acid 125-250 mg q6-12h
- [P1273] **HECHO** — Haloperidol and lorazepam can cause paradoxical agitation → decrease (not increase) dose or discontinue
- [P1274] **HECHO** — Antipsychotics in dementia patients: increased sensitivity to adverse effects (sedation, EPS); "black box" warning = increased risk sudden death and cerebrovascular events
