(7th Edition, 2022)

## Editorial synthesis — global challenges

- [P001] **HECHO** — since 6th ed. (2015), rapid communications/social media influential but unmonitored for veracity; can be counterproductive for public health goals
- [P002] **HECHO** — machine learning/AI redefining personal and public health practice globally; could bridge health disparities where infrastructure limited
- [P003] **HECHO** — new infectious agents emerged post-2015: Zika, MERS, acute flaccid paralysis (agent unknown), SARS-CoV-2
- [P004] **HECHO** — measles/mumps resurgence in industrialized countries caused by anti-vaccine social/political changes, not genetic/biologic agent changes
- [P005] **HECHO** — first time in recorded history human lifespan not expanding in many regions; possibly: healthcare access, unhealthy lifestyles, opioid overprescription, rising suicide/obesity
- [P006] **HECHO** — Africa youth bulge from reduced adult life expectancy (AIDS deaths 1990s-2000s) + successful post-2000 PMTCT efforts
- [P007] **HECHO** — political shift to conservatism; environmental safeguard regulations reversed, particularly US
- [P008] **HECHO** — climate change = perhaps major public health issue facing earth; impacts food supplies, habitable areas, infectious agent/vector distribution
- [P009] **HECHO** — global economy doing well on average but rich-poor disparity widening as rich get disproportionally richer
- [P010] **HECHO** — TNCs shifted manufacturing high-income → low-income countries; lower costs, minimal safety regulations
- [P011] **HECHO** — migration from low/middle-income and conflict countries increased; destination countries reluctant; underlying racial bias
- [P012] **HECHO** — intimate partner violence affects 35-40% women in some world regions
- [P013] **HECHO** — increasing drug resistance for TB/gonorrhoea; out of drugs for MDR-TB; caused by overprescription + commercial use of antibiotics → `antimicrobial resistance`
- [P014] **HECHO** — social media disseminates false anti-vaccine info; mothers refusing vaccination, lowering herd immunity; measles deaths soared in Samoa/Ukraine
- [P015] **HECHO** — killing of vaccinators in Pakistan/Afghanistan posed new challenge to polio eradication → `polio`
- [P016] **HECHO** — ≤25% total disability in some populations from mental health disorders; among adolescents in developed countries diagnosed proportion may reach 50%
- [P017] **HECHO** — mental health workers: median 9/100K globally; <1 in low-income vs 72 in high-income countries
- [P018] **HECHO** — genetics/genomics advances enable new disease study; gene alteration in human foetuses introduces complex ethical issues
- [P019] **ALCANCE** — SDGs cover communicable/non-communicable diseases, gender/economic/racial disparities, environmental drivers, human rights approach to health
- [P020] **HECHO** — SARS-CoV-2 (recognized Jan 2020): >3.25M cases, >226K deaths within 4 months; only proven mitigation = social distancing/global quarantine
- [P021] **HECHO** — WHO projects ~250K additional deaths/year 2030-2050 from climate change health effects

- [P022] **ALCANCE** — Volume 1: 3 sections — development of discipline, determinants of health/disease, public health policies/law/ethics
- [P023] **HECHO** — strong public health base key to meeting UN SDGs for health improvement + reduction of health inequalities
- [P024] **ALCANCE** — Section 1: 21st-century scope, history in developed countries, history in LMICs, middle-income transitions, globalization impacts
- [P025] **ALCANCE** — Section 2: biological/behavioural/social/environmental determinants; particular emphasis on social determinants role in health inequalities
- [P026] **ALCANCE** — Section 3: leadership, ethics (including genetic research), right to health, law, health policy in developing + developed countries

## Structure and themes

- [P027] **ALCANCE** — Volume 2: 4 sections — information systems, epidemiological/biostatistical approaches, social science techniques, environmental/occupational health
- [P028] **HECHO** — methodological advances needed for globalization, emerging infections, demographic/nutrition/physical activity transitions, climate change
- [P029] **HECHO** — basic birth/death data not routinely available for most low-income + many middle-income countries; major impediment to tracking SDG progress
- [P030] **DEFINICION** — epidemiology and biostatistics = core sciences of public health
- [P031] **HECHO** — social science techniques gaining importance; epidemiological data alone insufficient for effective public health policies/programmes
- [P032] **HECHO** — demography = underappreciated basic science; ageing of all populations (especially LMICs) critical for economic survival in 21st century

## Structure and themes

- [P033] **DEFINICION** — "public health = what we as society do collectively to assure conditions for people to be healthy" (Institute of Medicine, 1988)
- [P034] **ALCANCE** — Volume 3: 4 sections — major health problems (NCDs + communicable), prevention/control of hazards, population groups, public health functions
- [P035] **HECHO** — TB caused death of billions since 1900s; 6.3M newly notified TB cases worldwide 2016
- [P036] **HECHO** — malaria: ~500K deaths annually; reduction success stalled since 2015
- [P037] **HECHO** — 328M chronically infected with hepatitis B/C; 1M deaths annually → `hepatitis`
- [P038] **HECHO** — 5.4M under-5-year-olds died in 2017 → `child mortality`
- [P039] **HECHO** — 15% of world population has disability; responses must use social determinants framework
- [P040] **HECHO** — European colonization impacts reverberate across centuries, causing multiple health issues for Indigenous populations worldwide → `Indigenous health`
- [P041] **HECHO** — largest-ever adolescent cohort exists; health shaped by urbanization, migration, global communications, economic development, planetary sustainability

## Introduction — definitions

- [P042] **DEFINICION** — public health = "science and art of preventing disease, prolonging life, promoting health through organized efforts of society" (Acheson Report 1988)
- [P043] **DEFINICION** — public health goal = "biologic, physical, mental well-being of all members of society regardless of gender, wealth, ethnicity, sexual orientation, country, political views" (Detels 2003)
- [P044] **HECHO** — 1948 UN Declaration: health = basic human right
- [P045] **HECHO** — 17 SDGs declared 2012; focus on inequalities, poverty, climate change + areas essential for optimal public health
- [P046] **HECHO** — SDGs superseded Millennium Development Goals; underscore public health concerns beyond disease elimination

## Functions of public health

- [P047] **DEFINICION** — public health = process of mobilizing local/state/national/international resources to assure conditions where all people can be healthy (Detels & Breslow 2002)
- [P048] **HECHO** — 16 functions of public health (CDC 1994): prevent disease/injuries, promote healthy lifestyles, surveillance, investigate health problems, formulate/enforce policies, organize cost-effective services, reduce disparities, protect environment, disseminate health info, promote health, disaster preparedness, reduce violence/war, research, develop new methodologies, train workforce, evaluate programmes
- [P049] **HECHO** — US: behavioural patterns cause ~40% deaths; social circumstances 15%; environmental factors 5% (Schroeder 2007)
- [P050] **DEFINICION** — implementation science = field delineating barriers/facilitators for rapid translation of scientific advances → health practice/policy
- [P051] **HECHO** — international collaboration essential: data sharing, policy formulation, management of public health issues across globalized world
- [P052] **HECHO** — computational/AI advances greatly increase ability to analyse massive datasets with thousands of variables; machine learning for finding associations/patterns for targeted interventions

## Contemporary health issues — poverty

- [P053] **HECHO** — >3B people (>half world, 1/3 children) live below $2.50/day poverty line; 1.3B on <$1.25/day (World Bank 2012)
- [P054] **HECHO** — US 2016: 40.6M Americans below poverty level; highest among African-Americans/Hispanic-Americans (23.2%) → `US Census Bureau`
- [P055] **HECHO** — 844M people lack clean drinking water; ≥2B use faeces-contaminated water sources → `WHO`
- [P056] **HECHO** — world population 7B+ (2017); estimated >9B by 2050; growth flat in many areas but continuing in Africa

## Contemporary health issues — epidemiological transition

- [P057] **HECHO** — 20th century: transition from infectious/communicable diseases → NCDs as major disease burden
- [P058] **HECHO** — by early 21st century heart/chronic diseases = leading death causes; pneumonia/influenza no longer top 10
- [P059] **HECHO** — global longevity 71.4 years; population ageing from increased longevity + decreased birth rates; growth below replacement in many countries

## Measuring disease occurrence

- [P060] **DEFINICION** — YPLL (years of productive life lost): emphasizes diseases reducing productive lifespan (currently defined 75 years)
- [P061] **DEFINICION** — DALYs (disability-adjusted life years): emphasizes diseases compromising function + includes premature mortality
- [P062] **HECHO** — NCDs = 70% of disease deaths worldwide; cardiovascular diseases = half of NCD deaths
- [P063] **HECHO** — communicable diseases = major death cause only in Africa; account for ~30% global disease burden
- [P064] **HECHO** — WHO projected communicable diseases: decline from 39% → 30% of global DALYs by 2030
- [P065] **DEFINICION** — syndemicity (1990s): diseases occur in specific contexts; interactions between diseases affect vulnerability/severity; requires broader public health approach

## Communicable diseases

- [P066] **HECHO** — barriers to vaccine coverage: poverty, geographic obstacles, low education, logistics, civil unrest/wars, corruption, government mistrust
- [P067] **HECHO** — individual with communicable disease can circumnavigate globe while infectious but asymptomatic; SARS spread SE Asia → Canada within weeks
- [P068] **HECHO** — newly identified pathogens 1994-2019: SARS-CoV-2 (2019), acute flaccid myelitis (2018), Zika (2012), MERS-CoV (2012), H1N1 (2009), avian influenza human cases (2004), SARS (2003), Nipah (1999), H5N1 (1997), new variant CJD + Australian bat lyssavirus (1996), HHV-8 (1995), Savia + Hendra virus (1994)
- [P069] **HECHO** — SARS-CoV-2: recognized Jan 2020, spread from China → pandemic; >3.25M cases, >226K deaths in 4 months
- [P070] **HECHO** — NDM-1 (New Delhi metallo-beta-lactamase) encodes multiple-antibiotic resistance; spread rapidly to many countries via transmissible genetic elements
- [P071] **HECHO** — >1B people (1/7 world) suffer ≥1 tropical disease: Buruli ulcer, Chagas, cholera, dengue, dracunculiasis, trypanosomiasis, leishmaniasis, leprosy, lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, schistosomiasis, helminthiasis, trachoma
- [P072] **HECHO** — 20 factors for emergence/re-emergence of infectious diseases: demographic expansion into remote areas, proximity to animals, sanitary breakdown, land use changes, climate changes, behavioural changes, social inequality, international travel, food handling changes, pathogen evolution, drug resistance, vector resistance, immunosuppression, surveillance deterioration, illiteracy, lack of political will, biowarfare, war/unrest, famine, declining immunization
